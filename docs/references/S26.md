# Nine prime divisors in odd distinct covering systems

*Summary [S26] · M. Schroeder, Zenodo preprint, 13 Sep 2026 (v1.0.1, rev. 15 Sep), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22759614. Not peer-reviewed; third of a self-published series (ranks 7, 8, 9).*

*Summarized by: Claude Fable 5.1 for Ryan Wu. Status of our check: **unchecked** (Lean package not rebuilt here).*

**TL;DR.** Claims that every finite odd distinct covering system has at least nine distinct primes dividing the lcm $N$ of its moduli, with no restriction on exponents; hence $N \ge 3\cdot5\cdots29 = 3\,234\,846\,615$, and, by excluding the squarefree rank-9 case with a product-measure union bound, $N \ge 9\,704\,539\,845$. Also: any odd distinct family on at most eight primes leaves density $\ge 1/1\,002\,375$ uncovered.

## Method (as described)
Prime replacement (Simpson–Zeilberger) reduces to support $\{3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23\}$; a completion adds all pure prime powers and the mixed moduli $15,21,35,45,63,75,105,165$; "conditional reweighting" retaining the joint geometry of the 3- and 5-coordinates on 135 coarse cells; thresholds $(\tau_7,\dots,\tau_{23}) = (2,4,4,8,8,12)$; a convex vertex decomposition reduces continuous budgets to a finite certificate of 28 001 integer inequalities.
